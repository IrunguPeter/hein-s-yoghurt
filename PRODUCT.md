# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Plain static HTML and CSS in a single folder. No build step, no dependencies, no framework. Deployment target is undecided (the owner did not name a host), so the deliverable must be a folder that can be dropped onto any static host or opened locally as-is.

## Users

Neighbours and nearby residents, reached through a family or street WhatsApp group or a number passed along by someone who already buys. They arrive on a phone, usually inside the WhatsApp in-app browser, usually on mobile data. Most already know Hein personally; the small minority who do not need to establish trust in about three seconds.

The job to be done: "I want yogurt for the weekend and I want it to be the one Hein makes." A secondary job, common enough to design for: "I got this number — is it any good, what does it cost, and can I get some today?"

## Product Purpose

A one-page site whose entire job is to make two flavours, two pot sizes, real prices, and the way to order unmistakable in a few seconds, and then hand the visitor to a WhatsApp conversation. Success is a WhatsApp message that names a flavour and a size. Success for the owner is fewer clarifying texts — nobody should have to ask what the big pot costs, whether strawberry is in, or how to order.

## Positioning

Neighbour-made, ordered by message, collected or handed over in person. The trust is the person, not a brand system, and the buying moment is a conversation rather than a checkout. A neighbouring product could not truthfully copy the mechanism: the reason to buy is that Hein is the person who made it and is the person who answers the message.

The owner confirmed no differentiating claim he can currently back (no sourcing, ingredient, or production claims were offered). Positioning therefore rests only on what is factually true: two flavours, two sizes, real prices, one real person, one WhatsApp line.

## Operating Context

Orders arrive as WhatsApp messages, not through the site. The site has no cart, no account, no payment, and no order history; it lives in a phone contact and gets shared as a link in WhatsApp groups. After the message, the sale is arranged person to person. The site is a poster that can answer the three questions that arrive by text, and hand the rest to a human.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Flavour: vanilla and strawberry. Nothing else is offered.
- Size and price, both flavours at the same price: 250 ml — KSh 60; 500 ml — KSh 120.
- Currency is Kenyan shillings; prices are shown in whole shillings.
- Ordering is WhatsApp only. The page must never imply a cart, a checkout, or online payment.
- No photography exists. The design must be complete and convincing without an image of the product.
- The WhatsApp number, delivery or collection arrangement, delivery area and fee, and opening days are undecided. The page must present these as clearly marked fill-in slots rather than inventing them.
- Should be legible and fast on an older phone over mobile data; no heavy assets, no autoplay, no webfonts that block first paint.

## Brand Commitments

- The business is named after its maker: Hein. The brand reads as "Hein Yoghurt", a person's name attached to a plain noun.
- No logo, palette, type, or voice guide exists. None was supplied, and none is implied.
- The brand must be constructible from typography and layout alone, because no image assets will be available to carry it.
- Voice: plain, warm, and local. A neighbour describing what he makes, not a food brand narrating itself. No exclamation-mark abundance, no tasting notes, no health rhetoric.

## Evidence on Hand

- Real prices and pot sizes, confirmed by the owner: 250 ml KSh 60, 500 ml KSh 120, both flavours.
- The two flavour names and the WhatsApp order channel, confirmed by the owner.

Absent, and therefore never to be invented: any product photography; customer testimonials, names, or quoted messages; health, nutrition, or organic claims; sourcing or farm claims; reviews; certifications; a delivery fee or a named area; a business address, registration, or payment details; a second page of copy that implies a team or a shop.

## Product Principles

1. One action, one destination. WhatsApp is the checkout; every path on the page ends in the message thread and nothing else competes with it.
2. Say only what can be shown. Where there is no evidence, the page stays quiet rather than borrowing supermarket language to look more substantial.
3. The price is never a question. Both sizes and both flavours are on the page before the first scroll, because "how much?" is the most expensive text message Hein receives.
4. Phone-first, WhatsApp-shaped. Designed for a thumb, a small screen, mobile data, and an in-app browser that cannot be trusted to behave like a normal browser.
5. One person can run it. A folder the owner can open, read, and change a price in without a build step, a deploy pipeline, or anyone's permission.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific standard was established by the owner. Design defaults apply: WCAG AA contrast for text and controls, body copy at a comfortable mobile size, no information available only on hover, no meaning carried by colour alone (vanilla and strawberry must be distinguishable without relying on hue), full keyboard reachability, and tap targets sized for a thumb. Assume the reader may be outdoors in bright light on a low-end phone.
